You probably recognize one of these

Three groups I work with. Each one hits the same handful of decisions at roughly the same point in life.

Tech and biotech employees

Your stock vests faster than you can decide what to do with it.

You work in Kendall Square or along 128, and so does most of your net worth.

What we work through

  • Sell or hold RSUs when they vest
  • When to exercise ISOs, and how much AMT that triggers
  • Whether to buy ESPP shares, and when to sell them
  • How to bring down a concentrated stock position before or after a liquidity event
  • Life coverage sized to a young family when most of your net worth is one company's stock

Physicians, residency through attending

You started earning late and the decisions came all at once.

Whether you're a resident at a Boston teaching hospital or an attending a few years out, your plan hasn't caught up with your career.

What we work through

  • Own-occupation disability coverage sized to your specialty
  • Paying down student loans versus saving for retirement
  • The order to fund your 401(k) or 403(b), Roth IRA and HSA
  • What to do with the income jump after training

Small business owners

You run the business. Your own plan keeps getting pushed.

You have 5 to 50 people in MetroWest and either no retirement plan or one nobody has looked at in years.

What we work through

  • A standalone 401(k) versus joining a pooled employer plan
  • Plan design: who's eligible, how the match works, safe harbor or not
  • Whether you qualify for small-employer startup tax credits
  • How much to put toward your own retirement, and what happens to the business later
  • Buy-sell and key person coverage, so the business has a plan if an owner dies or becomes disabled

Why I do this

Most people my age don't have someone in their corner for the financial decisions that matter early on. I try to be that person.

My family came to this country from Iran in the '90s with $1,000. I grew up watching them build everything from scratch, and that shaped how I think about money, risk, and what it means to build something that lasts.

My parents came here for their PhDs. My father studied mechanical engineering at UConn and my mother studied microbiology at Clemson. I come from a family of engineers, scientists and doctors.

I started in aerospace engineering at the University of Alabama. During COVID I got into the markets, and I switched to finance and real estate. Engineers' kids end up in planning for the same reason they end up in engineering: you want to build something that lasts, and you check the math before you trust it.

I live in Newton with Rosie, my Pembroke Welsh Corgi. When I'm not working, I'm usually out for a run.
